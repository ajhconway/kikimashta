Scheduling

Adaptive scheduling uses ts-fsrs (MIT) and srs-kit (BSD-3-Clause). Their full license texts are included in the dependency notices. The initial model is FSRS-6 with shared parameters; personal parameter fitting will follow sufficient review history.

Japanese writing fonts

Writing practice uses Noto Sans JP and Noto Serif JP by Google/Adobe, and Klee One by Fontworks. These fonts are self-hosted under the SIL Open Font License, version 1.1. Full licenses: Noto Sans JP, Noto Serif JP, and Klee One. Provider-generated subsets cover the current word bank; source URLs, byte hashes, and glyph coverage are recorded in writing-font provenance.

Japanese audio

Practice and vocabulary-card speech is AI-generated with ElevenLabs, model Eleven v4. The project owner selected Otani, Morioki, Goro, Tsuki, Takeshi, Lime, Ritsuto, and Itsuki through word and sentence auditions. Every card has a recording in each voice. See the voice registry and generation and processing provenance.

Delivery recordings receive constant-gain volume matching toward −22 LUFS with true-peak headroom. Clips constrained by peaks or short-utterance measurement remain quieter and are listed for review. Original speech timing and provider recordings are preserved. New word pronunciation, regional accents, and playback still need listening review on the iPhone.

Earlier OpenAI audio

Earlier practice and catalog recordings use OpenAI gpt-realtime-2.1-mini with Marin and Cedar. Their original generation, volume processing and repair history remain in starter provenance and catalog provenance.

Earlier Open JTalk audio

Speech synthesized with Open JTalk 1.11 and HTS Voice NIT ATR503 M001 1.05, HTS Working Group. Voice copyright 2003–2012 Nagoya Institute of Technology and 2003–2008 Tokyo Institute of Technology, licensed under CC BY 3.0. The clips have been encoded as MP3, with outside silence trimmed and short padding added.

These earlier baseline clips are retained for reference and are no longer used in practice. See earlier audio provenance.

Voice comparison

The separate voice comparison uses AI-generated Japanese speech from OpenAI, model gpt-realtime-2.1-mini, with the Marin, Cedar, and Coral voices. Gain and silence padding were applied before MP3 encoding. See audition provenance.

The full-sentence comparison uses Marin and Cedar. Generation and processing records are in sentence provenance.

The earlier expanded vocabulary recordings used Marin and Cedar with pronunciation guides and word meanings supplied during generation. Original recordings, technical checks, and repair history are recorded in catalog audio provenance. Japanese pronunciation and pitch accent still need listening review.

Brand artwork

Brand artwork supplied by the project owner. The transparent cat mascot and cleaned icon were adapted with OpenAI’s built-in image editor. The supplied original is preserved; full edit prompts and asset records are in brand provenance.

The pace selector uses Minimal, Light, Moderate, High, and Intense versions of Kiku. Minimal reuses the original sleepy mascot; the other expressions were made with the built-in image editor while preserving the original character. Prompts, source references and file hashes are recorded in Kiku pace artwork provenance.

Pictures

Forty approved replacement illustrations were generated with OpenAI’s built-in image tool and delivered as compressed JPEGs. Selected originals, exact prompts, references and review history remain in the repository. See the active artwork provenance and its linked batch manifests.

The six original starter picture cards are original, AI-authored SVG illustrations created for Kikimashta. They retain their separate MIT license. Their design records are in vector provenance.

The expanded vocabulary library contains original AI-authored SVG scenes and symbols under AGPLv3 only, plus reused starter artwork. Context-dependent pictures are flagged for review. Generator source, scene descriptions, asset hashes, and license records are in catalog image provenance.

The earlier watercolor illustrations and their generation records are preserved in the repository. They were generated with OpenAI's image generation tool; they are no longer the current picture cards.
